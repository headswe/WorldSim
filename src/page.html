A whole planet

Every seed grows its own planet. Tectonic plates drift and collide to push up mountain ranges, prevailing winds carry rain inland and leave dry rain shadows behind the ridges, and the rain carves valleys and gathers into rivers that run through lakes and deltas to the sea. High on the slopes, springs feed thin brooks that tumble down the hillsides and join into streams and then rivers. Forests, grassland, taiga, tundra and desert follow from the latitude, the height and the rain. The world wraps around east to west, and the poles are icy.

Walking, swimming and flying

Walk the forest floor, run, and jump up single blocks. In water you swim slowly and can keep your head up by jumping. Flying lifts you above the trees and is fast, which is the easiest way to scout a valley or follow a river down to the coast. The corner readout shows your position, the time of day, the seed and the build.

Breaking and building

Aim at a block within a few steps to break it, or place the block chosen in the bar at the bottom: logs, planks, leaves, stone, mossy stone and water. Water you place behaves like the rivers: it spreads, flows downhill and pours over edges as waterfalls. Changes last while you stay in this world; growing a new world or reloading the page starts fresh.

Sailing

Stand at the shore facing open water and launch the boat. It is a ten metre sailing yacht that needs deep enough water, rides the same waves you see, and heels in the wind. Throttle forward and back and steer with the helm; press the boat key again close to it to step ashore, or to climb back aboard later.

The planet map

The map shows the whole planet with you on it. Switch between six views (terrain, plates, rain, temperature, drainage and rivers) and lay rivers, contours, plate edges, wind and storms, clouds, a grid and your own trail over any of them. Point at a place to read its biome, height and temperature. Ctrl+click (Cmd+click on a Mac) teleports you there.Double-tap a place to teleport there.

Weather

Clouds, wind and rain come from a running weather simulation over the planet around you. Storm systems drift across, cumulus build into thunderheads with lightning, rain darkens the sky and rings the water, and the wind sways the trees, carries the falling leaves and raises the sea from ripples to long storm swells. The Weather tab can push the climate warmer, wetter, cloudier, stormier or hazier, and puts it back as simulated in one click.

The weather follows the day: mornings start clear, cumulus build as the land warms, thunderstorms break in the late afternoon and the sky settles at dusk. Lows in the middle latitudes bring fronts: high wisps of cirrus first, then a thickening grey deck and steady rain, and later a line of towering cloud and downpours with cold, clearer, gusty air behind it. Wind comes in gusts, and a storm sends a cold rush of air out ahead of its rain.

The pieces feed each other. Storms live on what they can draw from: tropical storms need warm sea and die away over land, and lows in the middle latitudes deepen over the ocean, then fill and fade over a day or two. Thunderstorms grow, pour and collapse; the cold air under them spreads out as a gust front and sets off new storms where it meets warm, damp air. Sunny land makes sea breezes in the afternoon and the wind turns back offshore at night, cold air drains down the valleys on calm nights, and wind dropping off the far side of a range warms as it falls. Rain soaks the ground, wet ground feeds the next day's clouds and fog, and rivers and lakes run high and muddy after a wet spell and low after a dry one. Storms far out at sea send long swell to the coast, where it grows taller in the shallows and breaks on the beach. The corner readout shows the air pressure and whether it is rising or falling, and the map's wind layer marks the thunderstorms around you.

Rain or snow depends on how cold the air is, and the higher you climb the colder it gets. Snow settles while it falls and melts again in warm air and sun; above the snow line it lasts all season. Rain leaves the ground dark and shiny, with puddles in low spots, and it dries off afterwards. Fog drifts in off cold seas, gathers in the valleys after clear, calm nights and burns off in the morning sun, and on high ground a low wet sky wraps you in cloud. Showers can be seen as grey veils in the distance, and with the sun low behind you a rainbow can appear in the rain. The corner readout says what the weather is doing where you are.

Sound

You hear the rain on open ground and on the leaves above you, the wind rising and gusting, the trees rustling and the surf near the sea. Thunder rolls in after the flash, a few seconds later for every kilometre to the strike. Set the volume, or turn sound off, in the Weather tab.

Seasons and time

Spring brings blossom and fresh leaves, summer deep green, autumn scarlet and gold with leaves drifting down, and winter bare branches, snow wherever it is cold enough. The sun and moon cross the sky on a day of a set length, with stars at night; you can stop the clock or step it an hour at a time.

Looks and sharing

Four looks (Cinematic, Painted, Ink and Retro) change how the frame is graded. The World tab can share the spot you are standing on as a short code or link with the seed, view, time, season and weather, so someone else can open exactly that view.
